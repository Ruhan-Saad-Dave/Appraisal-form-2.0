**What's new here:** SoHSS is added under Dean of Non-Engineering. It follows the exact same flow as SoEMR — a Director, with multiple HODs underneath it (one per department), each HOD owning only the faculty in their own department. I haven't invented specific department names for SoHSS (unlike SoEMR's four named departments) since none were given — send those over and I'll slot them in the same way SoEMR's are listed.

Every teaching-staff form (Faculty, HOD, Director, Dean) is split into 5 parts — A, B, C, D, and E — all filled by the person submitting the form. Parts A, B, C, and E move through the normal hierarchy chain described below. Part E is the ACR portion and behaves exactly as the ACR process did before — it simply travels with A, B, C through the normal chain.

### Part D — routing and the release gate

Part D always goes straight to the **Registrar**, bypassing every level between the originator and the Registrar. The Registrar scores and approves it. But approval by the Registrar does **not** mean Part D goes straight to the VC anymore:

- **If the form's originator is Faculty, HOD, or Director** (i.e. anyone with a Dean — or, for CISR, a Center Head — sitting above them in the chain): once the Registrar approves Part D, it does **not** release to the VC immediately. It **waits** until the Dean (Engineering or Non-Engineering, matching the school) — or, for CISR, the Center Head — approves Parts A, B, C, and E of that same form. Only once that Dean/Center Head-level approval lands does Part D release to the VC, together with Parts A, B, C, and E.
- **If the form's originator is a Dean (or the Center Head for CISR)** — the top of the chain below the VC — there's no Dean/Center Head-level stage left to wait for. Part D releases to the VC as soon as the Registrar approves it, exactly as before.

Part D still never routes through the HOD, Director, or Dean for **scoring** at any point — the wait only holds up *release to the VC*, it doesn't change who scores Part D (only the Registrar ever does).

**Implementation note:** this means Part D needs its own status beyond "approved" — something like `registrar_approved_pending_release`, which flips to `released_to_vc` only when the corresponding Dean/Center Head-stage approval event fires on that same form's main chain.

CISR now follows this same Part A–E structure — see section 3 below, where the Center Head plays the role the Dean plays elsewhere (the final reviewer before the VC). **Non-Teaching Staff is not affected by any of this** and keeps its original single-track flow and transparency rules exactly as before.

### 1) For the Engineering Schools — SoCSEA, SoBB, SoCE, SoEMR

#### For Faculty:

When the faculty fills the form and submits it, Parts A, B, C, and E should go to the HOD. The form should go only to the HOD of the faculty's own department. Then the HOD inserts a score into Parts A, B, C, and E of the faculty form and submits it. After that, these parts get forwarded to the Director, and the form should go only to the Director of the faculty's own school. Then the Director inserts a score into Parts A, B, C, and E and submits it. After that, these parts get forwarded to the Dean, and the form should go only to the Dean of Engineering. Then the Dean inserts a score into Parts A, B, C, and E and submits it. Finally, these parts go to the VC, and the VC inserts a score into Parts A, B, C, and E and submits it.

Separately, Part D of the faculty form should go directly to the Registrar, bypassing the HOD, Director, and Dean entirely. The Registrar inserts a score into Part D and submits it. Once the Registrar approves Part D, it waits until the Dean of Engineering approves Parts A, B, C, and E of this same faculty form — only then does Part D release to the VC, together with Parts A, B, C, and E. Part D still never passes through the HOD or Director for scoring at any point.

#### For HOD:

When the HOD fills the form and submits it, Parts A, B, C, and E get forwarded to the Director, and the form should go only to the Director of the HOD's own school. Then the Director inserts a score into Parts A, B, C, and E of the HOD form and submits it. After that, these parts get forwarded to the Dean, and the form should go only to the Dean of Engineering. Then the Dean inserts a score into Parts A, B, C, and E and submits it. Finally, these parts go to the VC, and the VC inserts a score into Parts A, B, C, and E and submits it.

Separately, Part D of the HOD form should go directly to the Registrar, bypassing the Director and Dean. The Registrar inserts a score into Part D and submits it. Once the Registrar approves Part D, it waits until the Dean of Engineering approves Parts A, B, C, and E of this same HOD form — only then does Part D release to the VC, together with Parts A, B, C, and E.

#### For Director:

When the Director fills the form and submits it, Parts A, B, C, and E get forwarded to the Dean, and the form should go only to the Dean of Engineering. Then the Dean inserts a score into Parts A, B, C, and E of the Director form and submits it. Finally, these parts go to the VC, and the VC inserts a score into Parts A, B, C, and E and submits it.

Separately, Part D of the Director form should go directly to the Registrar, bypassing the Dean. The Registrar inserts a score into Part D and submits it. Once the Registrar approves Part D, it waits until the Dean of Engineering approves Parts A, B, C, and E of this same Director form — only then does Part D release to the VC, together with Parts A, B, C, and E.

#### For Dean:

When the Dean fills the form and submits it, Parts A, B, C, and E get forwarded to the VC, and the VC inserts a score into Parts A, B, C, and E of the Dean form and submits it.

Separately, Part D of the Dean form goes directly to the Registrar. The Registrar inserts a score into Part D and submits it. After the Registrar approves it, Part D goes directly to the VC.

### Transparency in Flow:

In the VC dashboard, teaching appraisal cards and the opened VC review form show an "Average Score" before the VC score, calculated from the available Parts A, B, C, and E scores submitted before the VC stage (the self/faculty score and the applicable prior reviewer scores — HOD, Director, Dean — in that chain). This score summary is displayed to one decimal place; it is not shown when the form's originator is a Dean, since there's no prior reviewer stage below the Dean to average.

ExpDyp, ExpPrev, and ExpTotal are collapsed into a single "Experience" field for display clarity in the Faculty Information section — and this applies across every reviewer's screen (HOD, Director, Dean, VC, and self), not just the VC's.

The score-hiding rule described below (each reviewer sees only the self-score, not intermediate reviewers' scores, until the VC) holds for the reviewer forms checked; it wasn't separately re-verified on every single reviewer screen, so treat it as the standing rule and flag it if any screen is ever found showing more than it should.

#### For Faculty:

When the faculty fills the form and submits it, Parts A, B, C, and E should go to the HOD — only the HOD of the faculty's own department. The HOD inserts a score into Parts A, B, C, and E and submits it. After that, these parts get forwarded to the Director, and the Director should not be able to see the scores given by the HOD — the Director should only be able to see the faculty's self-scores. Then the Director inserts a score and submits it. ## 5) IMPLEMENTED — Role Ownership Transfer (HOD, Director, Dean)

**Status: this section is now implemented, including UI — not backend-only as originally scoped.** See "What's actually built" below for the gap between the original spec and current code.

**What this covers:** an authority-role holder (HOD, Director, or Dean) can be replaced by a newly appointed person mid-cycle. When this happens, the outgoing person's ownership of that role is transferred to the incoming person, and the outgoing person reverts to being a Faculty member (submitting their own appraisal as Faculty going forward, routed to whichever HOD/Director/Dean now covers them).

### What's actually built

Contrary to this section's original framing as "backend/data-model only," there is a real, working UI on top of this:
- A VC-facing screen for transferring Director (per school) and Dean (per Dean track) ownership, showing the current holder(s) and a "Transfer" action with a confirmation dialog.
- A Director-facing flow for managing their own school — creating departments/programs, creating HODs, assigning faculty — which also handles HOD transfer/removal and HOD account deactivation.

### Core principle: a role is an assignable position, not a fixed attribute of a person

Today, if "HOD of Mechanical Engineering" is just a field directly on a person's account, transferring ownership means editing that person's account — which is fragile and loses history. Instead, treat each authority role as its own **assignable position** with a start/end record, and have a person "hold" that position for a period of time.

### Data model

```
RoleAssignment
├── assignment_id
├── role_type         ("HOD" | "Director" | "Dean")
├── scope_type         ("department" | "school" | "dean_type")
├── scope_id            (department_id, school_id, or "Engineering"/"Non-Engineering")
├── user_id             (the person currently holding this position)
├── status              ("active" | "transferred")
├── start_date
└── end_date            (null while active)
```

**As actually used today**, this is narrower than the target model above: the assignment id, role type, scope, the holder's email/name, and the start date. The active/inactive status and end date aren't yet surfaced anywhere in the UI, and which "scope" a role applies to (department for HOD, school for Director, Dean-track for Dean) is inferred by convention from the role type rather than stored as its own explicit field. This may still exist further back in the system; it's just not something the UI shows or relies on yet.

Department, School, and Dean-type records no longer store a direct `hod_id` / `director_id` / `dean_id` foreign key to a user. Instead, routing always resolves through the **currently active** `RoleAssignment` row for that scope:

- Faculty's HOD = the active `RoleAssignment` where `role_type = "HOD"` and `scope_id = faculty.department_id`
- Department's Director = the active `RoleAssignment` where `role_type = "Director"` and `scope_id = department.school_id`
- School's Dean = the active `RoleAssignment` where `role_type = "Dean"` and `scope_id = school.dean_type`

### What happens on a transfer

1. An admin action (whatever surface triggers it later) specifies: `role_type`, `scope_id`, outgoing `user_id`, incoming `user_id`.
2. The outgoing person's `RoleAssignment` row is closed: `status = "transferred"`, `end_date = now()`.
3. A new `RoleAssignment` row is created for the incoming person: same `role_type`/`scope_id`, `status = "active"`, `start_date = now()`.
4. The outgoing person's account role reverts to **Faculty** — this only removes their approval/write permission for that position going forward. Their own future appraisal forms now route through whichever HOD/Director/Dean currently holds authority over them. Their historical activity (own past appraisals, and forms they reviewed while holding the role) is untouched and remains permanently accessible to them in read-only form — see the dedicated rule below.
5. Any faculty/department previously routed to the outgoing person now automatically routes to the incoming person, because routing always resolves via the *active* `RoleAssignment`, not a static link.

### Rules that must hold regardless of implementation details

- **Only one active `RoleAssignment` per scope at a time** — **not enforced today.** This is a known, called-out gap: nothing currently stops two accounts from both holding the same role for the same scope (e.g. two Directors for one school), and the transfer screen shows a visible warning banner when it detects more than one current holder for a scope, telling the admin it needs manual cleanup — transferring one of them doesn't resolve the duplicate on its own. So this remains a target invariant, not a guaranteed one yet.
- **Historical integrity is untouched.** Any appraisal form already scored by the outgoing person keeps that person's name/ID as the reviewer of record. A transfer never rewrites who reviewed a past form — it only changes who reviews *future* ones.
- **Snapshot the reviewer at submission time.** Every appraisal form should store the specific `user_id` (not just the role) who acted at each stage, resolved from the active `RoleAssignment` at the moment the form reached that stage. This is what protects historical records from a later transfer.
- **The outgoing person's own faculty appraisal chain must be re-resolved.** The moment they revert to Faculty, they need a department assignment (if they don't already have one from before they were promoted) so their own future appraisal form routes correctly.
- **Demotion removes write/approval permission only — it never deletes or alters historical data.** See the dedicated rule below.

### Target rule (not yet implemented in the frontend): historical data is never deleted, moved, or edited on transfer — read-only access survives demotion

**Status check:** no evidence of this was found built out yet. Scores are still stored per-role on the form (an HOD score, a Director score, a Dean score, a VC score) rather than snapshotted against the specific person who acted, and there's no "look up my past reviews as HOD/Director/Dean" screen anywhere — the closest existing thing shows one form's own approval history, not a person's cross-year reviewing history. Treat everything below as the intended design, not a shipped guarantee, until the snapshot behavior and the email+academic-year lookup actually exist.

When a person is transferred out of a role (HOD, Director, or Dean), what actually changes is narrow: **their permission to act as an approver in that role going forward is revoked.** Nothing about their past activity is touched.

**What stays exactly as it was, forever, regardless of any later transfer:**
- Every appraisal form they scored while holding that role — same scores, same comments, same reviewer name, same timestamp, same academic year, same form status. A transfer event never deletes, rewrites, reassigns, or re-attributes a single field on a historical form.
- Their name/identity as "reviewer of record" on every stage they acted on, in every academic year, permanently — this was already required by the "Snapshot the reviewer at submission time" rule above, and it's exactly what makes the guarantee in this section possible.

**What the demoted person can still do, indefinitely, after becoming Faculty again:**
- Look up and view (read-only) every appraisal form tied to their own identity from any previous academic year — whether they were the *originator* (their own self-appraisal from that year) or the *reviewer* (forms they scored as HOD/Director/Dean that year).
- This lookup is keyed by **email ID + academic year** — given their email and a chosen academic year, the system returns every record tied to that email for that year, regardless of what role they held back then or what role (Faculty) they hold now.
- This is a strictly **read-only** view. Once demoted, they cannot edit, re-score, comment on, resubmit, or in any way modify a historical form — not their own past self-appraisals, and not forms they previously reviewed as HOD/Director/Dean. The write/approval permission is exactly what was revoked; the read/history permission is exactly what was preserved.

**Why this must not depend on the `RoleAssignment` table:** `RoleAssignment` only tracks *who currently* holds a position — after a transfer, the outgoing person's row is `status = "transferred"`, so re-deriving "what did this person review" by looking at *today's* RoleAssignment state would silently lose their history. That's why each form's stage record must independently store the acting `reviewer_user_id` and `academic_year` at the time it was scored (per the snapshot rule above) — historical lookups query these stored fields directly on the form records themselves, never by re-resolving current role ownership.

**Minimum fields each form/review-stage record needs to make this work:**

```
FormReviewStage
├── form_id
├── stage_role         ("HOD" | "Director" | "Dean" | "Registrar" | "VC" | ... )
├── reviewer_user_id    (snapshot — who actually acted, permanent, never rewritten)
├── academic_year
├── score / status
└── acted_at
```

A query like *"show me every form where `reviewer_user_id` = this person's ID, for `academic_year` = 2024–25"* is then all that's needed to serve the demoted person's history — completely independent of whatever role they hold today, and independent of whoever holds that position now.

### Rule: in-progress forms at the moment of transfer

If a form is sitting unscored in the outgoing HOD/Director/Dean's queue at the moment of transfer, it **moves immediately to the newly appointed person** — the position owns the queue, not the individual. This is the stated promise of the transfer screen itself ("any in-progress forms in their queue move to the incoming person automatically"), and it lines up with how routing already resolves the current reviewer dynamically rather than pinning a form to a specific person. Concretely:

- Any form still awaiting review at that scope, where the current stage's reviewer resolves to the outgoing person, is automatically re-resolved to the incoming person as soon as the transfer goes through — no manual reassignment step needed, since routing already resolves via whoever currently holds the role rather than a static link (see "What happens on a transfer" above).
- This applies only to forms **not yet scored** at that stage. Anything the outgoing person already scored and submitted before the transfer keeps their name as reviewer of record, per the historical-integrity rule above — a transfer never rewrites completed work, only reassigns what's still pending.
